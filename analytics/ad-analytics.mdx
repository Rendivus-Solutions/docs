Ad Analytics is a performance dashboard for your connected Meta Ads accounts. It answers "how are my ads doing this month, compared with last month?" and lets you act on the answer without opening Ads Manager.

**Where:** sidebar → **Ad Analytics**. **Cost:** 0 credits. **Plans:** Professional and VIP. Requires a [connected Meta account](/connections/connect-meta).

## Pick what you are looking at

The filter row at the top of the page sets everything below it:

- **Date range**: the last 7, 14, 30 or 90 days. The page opens on the last 30 days.
- **Compare to**: the previous period of the same length, or no comparison. "Same period last year" appears once Raddivus holds a year of history for the account.
- **Channel**: all channels, or one (Facebook, Instagram, and so on).
- **Ad account**: shows which account the numbers come from. A brand with two or more Meta ad accounts opens on the first one; pick another, or **All ad accounts** to add them together. An account that needs reconnecting says so here.

Your choices stay in the page address, so a refresh or a link you share opens the same view.

The period ends on the last day Meta's numbers are fully in. That is normally yesterday. In the evening (US time), before the nightly refresh, it ends the day before, so a half-finished day never makes your numbers look like they dropped. The chart, its legend, the "through" line under it and the card changes always name the same last day.

<Note>
  The first time you open an account, Raddivus collects its last 180 days from
  Meta. This takes a moment, and the page says so while it runs. After that,
  the numbers refresh every night.
</Note>

## Headline cards and the trend chart

Each card shows one number for the period and how it changed against the comparison period, in green when the change is good for you and red when it is not. Click a card to draw that number in the chart below, day by day, with the comparison period as a second line.

Results count one kind of result at a time: the one your account spends the most on, such as **Leads** for a lead-generation account. Leads are never added to clicks. An account that reports online sales also sees revenue cards (Revenue, Ad spend, ROAS, New customers, Cost per new customer).

Money is shown in the ad account's own currency. Currencies that Meta counts in whole units, such as Japanese yen and Korean won, show no decimals. Under **All ad accounts**, accounts that use different currencies show amounts without a currency symbol.

## Channels and services

Two boxes split the period:

- **Channels** lists every place Meta ran your ads (Facebook, Instagram, Threads, WhatsApp, Messenger, Audience Network) with what each spent, its cost per result and its share of the spend. Anything Meta cannot place is **Other**, always last.
- **Services** groups your results by the brand's services ("Leads by service"). Each campaign's service is guessed from its name every night. A campaign whose name names no service is **Not assigned**, always last.

To set a campaign's service yourself, open the **Campaigns** tab and pick it from the campaign's **Service** menu. The Services box updates at once, any teammate can change it, and a service you picked is never replaced by the nightly guess. If the brand has no services yet, the box offers a button to add them on the brand page.

### Assign your campaigns

When more than half of the period's spend is **Not assigned**, the Services box shows a short note with **Assign campaigns** and **Dismiss**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click Assign campaigns">
    A window lists the unassigned campaigns that spent, biggest spender first,
    each with its Service menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a service for each">
    Every pick saves at once, and the Services box behind the window updates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Done">
    The note is gone for good for that ad account, for your whole team.
    **Dismiss** does the same without assigning anything. Closing the window
    with the X keeps the note, so you can come back to it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The note shows only when one ad account is selected.

## The table: campaigns, ad sets and ads

Below the boxes, tabs list **Suggestions**, **Campaigns**, **Ad sets** and **Ads**. Each row shows the item's on/off switch, its name, its budget per day (or a quality grade for ads), spend, results, cost per result, clicks and CTR for the period, with the change against the comparison period. A short grey note under the name says when something other than the item's own switch is stopping it (Campaign off, Ad set off, Scheduled, In review, Issues).

- **Search** by name, **filter** by status (Active, Paused, Scheduled, Issues) and **sort** by any number column.
- **Hide columns** you do not need from the column picker. Clicks and CTR start hidden. Your choice is remembered in this browser.
- Under **All ad accounts**, each row names its account.
- On the **Ads** tab, click a thumbnail to see it larger.

The table always lists everything Meta reports for the period, whatever channel is picked, and says so when a channel is picked.

## Turn campaigns, ad sets and ads on or off

Press a row's switch to turn that campaign, ad set or ad on or off on Meta. Raddivus asks you to confirm first, because turning something off stops its spending right away. If Meta refuses, the switch moves back and a message says why.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tick the rows">
    To change several at once, tick their boxes. The search row is replaced by
    a bar with the count.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Turn off or Turn on">
    Raddivus acts only on the ticked rows that would actually change, up to 50
    at a time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    One confirmation names how many will change. Any rows Meta refuses are put
    back and named together in one message.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Only the workspace owner or an admin can use the switches and tick boxes.
  Other teammates can see them, with a line above the table explaining why they
  cannot use them.
</Warning>

## Suggestions

The **Suggestions** tab lists the brand's weekly Raddivus recommendations, the same ones as the dashboard card, one per row with its category and the day it was written. The tab's number counts the suggestions still waiting for a decision. It turns amber when the oldest of them has waited more than two weeks; hover it to see since when. When none are left it says "You are all caught up."

**Apply** queues a suggestion and keeps it on the list, checked off. The **X** dismisses it. Acting on a suggestion here or on the dashboard is the same action.

### How the weekly recommendations are written

Every week, Raddivus judges each running Meta ad: **winning**, **holding steady**, **underperforming**, or **too early to judge**. For a winning or underperforming ad it names the most likely reason (the angle, the message, the visual, the copy, the audience, or for videos the first 3 seconds, the middle, or the ending) and proposes 1 to 5 tests to run next, such as a new creative or a new angle. Each recommendation cites a real number from your account, never a vague suggestion.

Ads whose goal is attention rather than customers (reach, impressions, link clicks, landing page views, engagement, likes, video views) are judged on their own terms. They read **Cheap**, **Typical** or **Expensive** plus what they buy, for example "Cheap reach" or "Expensive clicks", never "winning", and Raddivus never suggests putting more money into a cheap one, because it did not bring a lead or a sale. Every cost names its real unit (cost per lead, cost per 1,000 people reached), never a vague "cost per result". Website-lead ads are compared with your other lead ads, and video ads get hook, middle and ending advice even when Meta hides their type.

Ads that are too new or have too little data never get a verdict. They are grouped into one **Give it time** note, which you can dismiss. An ad is judged once it has run 3 days and has 1,000 views plus 3 results, or has spent twice your usual cost per result for that kind of result.

The ad grades on the Ads tab, the weekly recommendations and the [AI agent](/ai-agent/overview) all use these same rules, so they give the same verdict about the same ad.

Applying a recommendation queues it for us. Automatic execution is coming in an upcoming update, so for now applying tells us which moves you approve of.

When you dismiss a recommendation, Raddivus asks why, right on the recommendation. You can skip the question; the recommendation is dismissed either way. Your answer is remembered for the brand:

- A test you already ran is never suggested again for that same ad. Instead, the ad gets a different test for the same problem the next week (for a weak video opening: a question, a number, the finished result). The same test may still be suggested for a different ad.
- An idea you called not relevant is not suggested again for this brand.
- A number you said looks wrong is not built on again.

Any teammate's answer counts for the whole brand.

<Note>
  The four reasons you can pick: **Already tested this**, **Not relevant for this brand / ad**, **The numbers look wrong**, and **Other**, which lets you write a short note in your own words.
</Note>

Weekly recommendations are free on every plan, including the trial. They require a connected, active Meta ad account. 0 credits.

## Recent account changes

A history feed shows what changed on the chosen ad account recently: budget updates, ads created, ads paused or turned on, and targeting edits. Pick the last 7, 14 or 30 days and switch between **Key changes** and **All changes**. Under **All ad accounts** the feed is hidden, because it reads one account at a time.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The page says it is collecting history">
    Raddivus is reading the account's last 180 days from Meta for the first
    time. Wait a moment and refresh.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My numbers end the day before yesterday">
    Meta's numbers for yesterday arrive with the nightly refresh. Until then
    the period ends on the last complete day, so nothing looks lower than it
    is. Check again later in the night.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A campaign is under Not assigned">
    Its name does not name one of the brand's services. Pick the right service
    from the campaign's Service menu on the Campaigns tab.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A switch moved back on its own">
    Meta refused the change; the message beside it says why. If it asks you to
    reconnect, use its Reconnect button and try again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I cannot use the switches">
    Only the workspace owner or an admin can turn ads on or off. Ask one of
    them to change your role, or to make the change.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="No rows are showing">
    The search or status filter may be too narrow. Clear them and confirm the
    account has campaigns in Meta for the period.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
